\begin{introbox}{Research Question}
{\Large\bfseries Which factors best predict whether an MT metric agrees with
human judgments across resource tiers, translation directions, morphological
types, and domains?}
\begin{posteritems}
  \item \textbf{Translation direction is the strongest signal.}
\end{posteritems}
\end{introbox}

\begin{sectionbox}{How Is the Benchmark Built?}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.80\linewidth]{figures/pipeline_poster.pdf}

\raggedright
\begin{posteritems}
  \item \textbf{MQM} means Multidimensional Quality Metrics and categorizes
        errors by type and severity. \textbf{DA} means Direct Assessment and
        gives each sentence a human score. \textbf{NLLB} means No Language Left
        Behind, the model that generated the synthetic translations.
  \item Ten metrics are evaluated with segment-level Spearman $\rho$ against
        human or exploratory synthetic quality judgments.
\end{posteritems}
\end{sectionbox}

\boxsep

\begin{sectionbox}{What Best Predicts Metric Reliability?}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.65\linewidth]{figures/direction_morphology_poster.pdf}

\vspace{0.2em}
\begin{columns}[c,totalwidth=\linewidth]
  \begin{column}{0.31\linewidth}\centering
    {\Large\bfseries\color{byuRoyal}$\eta^2_{\rm dir}=\EtaSqDir$}\\
    {\normalsize direction}
  \end{column}
  \begin{column}{0.31\linewidth}\centering
    {\Large\bfseries\color{bodyGray}$\eta^2_{\rm morph}=\EtaSqMorph$}\\
    {\normalsize morphology}
  \end{column}
  \begin{column}{0.31\linewidth}\centering
    {\Large\bfseries\color{amanaGreen}$\eta^2_{\rm cell}=\EtaSqInteract$}\\
    {\small combined direction$\times$morphology cells}
  \end{column}
\end{columns}

\raggedright
\begin{posteritems}
  \item \textbf{Translation direction explains more than four times as much
        variance as morphology} in the like-for-like main-effect comparison.
  \item $\eta^2_{\rm cell}$ combines direction, morphology, and their cell
        structure in one factor, so its larger value is expected.
  \item English$\rightarrow$Chinese was scored after the benchmark analysis
        and is not included in this figure (COMET $\rho=\EnZhCOMETRho$).
  \item \textbf{Agglutinative} stacks distinct affixes, e.g., Turkish.
        \textbf{Fusional} merges several features in one affix, e.g., German.
        \textbf{Isolating} relies more on separate words, e.g., Chinese.
\end{posteritems}
\end{sectionbox}

\boxsep

\begin{sectionbox}{Why Do Resource-Tier Results Reverse?}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.76\linewidth]{figures/tier_bar_poster.pdf}

\raggedright
\begin{posteritems}
  \item Medium-tier evaluation datasets show stronger correlations across all
        ten metrics.
  \item \textbf{Tiers describe benchmark coverage, not languages themselves.}
        Annotation protocol and multi-year pooling are confounded with tier,
        so treat the reversal as descriptive.
\end{posteritems}
\end{sectionbox}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\linewidth]{figures/language_heatmap_poster.pdf}

\begin{warningbox}[equal height group=failurecases]{Why Does Ukrainian Collapse?}
\begin{posteritems}
  \item COMET correlation: $\rho=\LangCOMETUK$.
  \item Same-year Czech$\rightarrow$English data are mostly news and also near zero.
  \item \textbf{This points to low-signal WMT22 crowd labels rather than domain.}
\end{posteritems}
\end{warningbox}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.02\linewidth}\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.49\linewidth}
\begin{warningbox}[equal height group=failurecases]{Why Does Russian Look Weak?}
\begin{posteritems}
  \item Within-year correlations are $\RuWmtOneRho$ (WMT21) and
        $\RuWmtTwoRho$ (WMT22). Pooled correlation is $\LangCOMETRU$.
  \item \textbf{Simpson's Paradox: combining years with different average
        quality hides the stronger pattern inside each year.}
\end{posteritems}
\end{warningbox}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
